# Summary Report: “FCAJ x Agentic AI Build Week: Show Up. Build. Pitch. WIN!”

### Event Objectives

- Present projects created during Agentic AI Build Week.
- Share how teams selected problems, built MVPs, tested their systems, and prepared demonstrations.
- Help FCAJ members understand Agentic AI and the hackathon environment.
- Share practical lessons about teamwork, time management, and product pitching.

### Key Highlights

#### Building an Agentic AI product

Teams explained how they moved from identifying a problem and target users to designing a solution and building a working prototype. An effective project does not need many technologies; it needs to solve a clear problem and demonstrate value through a reliable user flow.

#### Defining the MVP

- Prioritize core functions that can be completed within the available time.
- Complete one main user flow before adding secondary features.
- Integrate and test early instead of postponing issues until the end.
- Prepare demo data and a backup plan.

#### Architecture and system integration

The teams presented their high-level architectures, the role of AI agents, data flows, and cloud services. These presentations clarified how frontend, backend, databases, AI services, and deployment infrastructure combine into a complete product.

The projects demonstrated several practical AI applications, including tools for non-technical users, cloud architecture and cost estimation for Solutions Architects, crowd-density monitoring with **YOLO** and **ByteTrack**, and financial fraud detection.

The presentations showed how AI tools such as Kiro can accelerate analysis, coding, and problem-solving. However, teams still need to identify the correct user **pain point**, choose a suitable solution, and validate AI-generated results.

Enterprise AI solutions also need to establish trust through three important controls:

- **Security:** Protect data and control access.
- **Monitoring:** Track system activity and detect anomalies.
- **Human-in-the-loop:** Keep people involved in verification and important decisions.

The smart-camera project also demonstrated practical challenges when processing real-time data, especially unstable network connectivity. Its architecture therefore needs to consider interruptions, latency, and recovery when connectivity is unreliable.

#### Hackathon teamwork

- Assign responsibilities according to each member’s strengths.
- Share progress frequently and integrate components early.
- Support one another when errors or changing requirements arise.
- Stay focused on the most valuable part of the product.
- Use Git correctly to reduce conflicts and lost changes when several members develop together.
- Define the work scope clearly and prepare the demonstration plan early.

#### Demo and pitch preparation

A good demo should be short, clear, and reliable. A strong pitch focuses on the problem, users, solution, value, and actual results instead of simply listing technologies.

The product should be deployed with a working end-to-end flow to demonstrate practical value. Teams should prepare input data, verify APIs and supporting services, and keep a backup plan for system or network failures.

### Key Takeaways

- An AI agent can analyze goals, select tools, and perform multiple steps instead of only answering questions.
- Projects should begin with a real user problem, not with a desire to use a particular technology.
- MVP scope must match the team’s available time, skills, and resources.
- A small, stable product is more convincing than a large but incomplete system.
- Communication, clear responsibilities, and early testing strongly affect team performance.
- Demonstrations and pitches are essential for communicating product value.
- Project scope should remain small enough to complete an MVP within the limited timeframe; expanding too early increases delays and errors.
- A working deployment is needed to prove that the solution can provide practical value.
- Security, monitoring, and human-in-the-loop controls improve trust in enterprise AI applications.
- Hackathons should be approached as opportunities to gain experience, learn, and connect without excessive pressure about awards.

### Event Experience

The event gave me a practical view of participating in a hackathon. Behind a short demonstration are many activities, including problem selection, architecture design, task assignment, integration, testing, and debugging.

The message **Show Up – Build – Pitch – Win** was especially meaningful: participate actively, turn ideas into products, communicate their value, and grow through the experience. The event also motivated me to continue learning AWS, explore Agentic AI, and participate in practical projects.
